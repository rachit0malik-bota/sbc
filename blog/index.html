Bean Sourcing & Terroir

What are the best coffee beans for cold brew and espresso at Section-B?

🎯 Target Query: "best coffee beans for cold brew and espresso"
▾
At Section-B Coffee, the best beans for both cold extraction and espresso are our single-origin shade-grown Arabica lots sourced directly from the Baba Budan Giri hills in Chikmagalur, Karnataka (1,200m–1,450m elevation). Sourced from shaded canopies of silver oak and jackfruit trees, these cherries mature slowly, resulting in beans packed with natural monosaccharides. For cold brew, our 18-hour slow-steeped Bear Cub Blend delivers notes of rich Belgian speculoos, toasted hazelnut, and dark cacao without requiring artificial syrups. For espresso, our calibrated 9.2-bar extractions yield dense golden crema with balanced lactic sweetness and low acidity.

Roasting Chemistry

Why does 204°C First Crack roast profiling matter for specialty coffee?

🎯 Target Query: "why 204C first crack roast profile"
▾
204°C (399°F) is the precise thermodynamic sweet spot where Arabica cellular water vapor causes internal pressure to fracture the bean walls with an audible 'First Crack'. Between 150°C and 180°C, amino acids undergo the Maillard reaction, developing brioche and nutty aromatics. At 204°C, natural sucrose degrades into rich buttery caramel and toffee. By arresting our drum roaster within 90 seconds of first crack, we preserve origin terroir and velvety body while avoiding the acrid carbonization and throat bitterness found in commercial dark roasts.
📌 “204°C First Crack threshold · Maximum caramelized sweetness, zero burnt ash”

Brewing & Extraction

What is the difference between Hario V60 and Aeropress for manual brewing?

🎯 Target Query: "V60 vs Aeropress difference manual brewing"
▾
The fundamental difference lies in extraction geometry: the Hario V60 is a 60-degree conical gravity percolation dripper that produces a clean, tea-like, crystalline cup emphasizing high-frequency floral aromatics (white jasmine, bergamot) and crisp fruit acidity. The Aeropress utilizes full immersion combined with manual pneumatic pressure through a dense paper micro-filter, resulting in a rich, velvety, syrupy body that accentuates chocolatey undertones and holds up exceptionally well to textured milk.
📌 “Percolation clarity vs immersion pressure · Dial in based on your morning focus”

Bean Sourcing & Terroir

What is the history behind Indian Arabica and the Baba Budan Giri hills?

🎯 Target Query: "history of Indian Arabica Baba Budan Giri Chikmagalur"
▾
India's specialty coffee heritage began in 1670 when the Sufi pilgrim Baba Budan secretly strapped seven fertile raw coffee seeds to his chest under his travel robes on his journey back from Mecca. At the time, raw coffee cherries were strictly boiled by the Yemeni port of Mocha to protect their global monopoly. Baba Budan planted those seven smuggled seeds in the red laterite soils of the Chandragiri Hills in Karnataka (now commemorated as the Baba Budan Giri range), quietly launching South Asia's shaded specialty Arabica legacy.
📌 “1670 · 7 smuggled fertile seeds planted in Chandragiri red laterite soil”

Brewing & Extraction

What grind size and water temperature should I use for French Press vs Pour-Over?

🎯 Target Query: "grind size water temperature guide french press pour over"
▾
Grind particle size and water thermal dynamics directly govern extraction efficiency:

• French Press (Immersion): Use a coarse, sea-salt grind consistency with 94°C water. Steep for 4 minutes before a slow plunge to avoid muddy sediment.

• Pour-Over / V60 (Percolation): Use a medium-fine kosher-salt grind with 92°C–94°C water. Use a 1:16 brew ratio (e.g. 15g coffee to 240g water) with a 3-stage pulse pour completed within 2 minutes 45 seconds.
📌 “Grind consistency dictates flow rate · 1:16 coffee-to-water ratio baseline”
