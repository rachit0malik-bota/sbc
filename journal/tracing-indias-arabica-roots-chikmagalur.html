The 1670 Smuggled Seeds: Tracing India’s Arabica Roots to Chikmagalur

How a pilgrim smuggled seven raw coffee seeds out of Yemen into Karnataka’s Baba Budan Giri hills, quietly launching South Asia’s specialty coffee legacy.

✍️
Arpit · Founder (Desk 14) Monday, October 12, 2026
✦ Baba Budan · 7 raw seeds strapped under travel robes in 1670

Long before commercial espresso machines and cold extraction towers existed, the global coffee trade was fiercely locked behind the port of Mocha in Yemen. Raw fertile coffee cherries were strictly boiled or parched to prevent foreign propagation. To carry a live seed abroad was punishable by death.

In the spring of 1670, an Indian Sufi pilgrim known as Baba Budan made his journey home from Mecca. Driven by reverence for the restorative black broth, he strapped seven fertile green coffee beans secretly against his chest under his travel robes — seven, a sacred number in Sufi tradition.

The Sanctuary of Chikmagalur

When Baba Budan reached the misty, rain-drenched Western Ghats in southwest Karnataka, he planted those seven smuggled seeds into the mineral-rich, red laterite soils of the Chandragiri Hills (now commemorated as the Baba Budan Giri range). The high-altitude canopy, dense cloud cover, and seasonal monsoon rains created a microclimate unlike anywhere else in the world.

“Indian shade-grown Arabica develops slowly under native silver oak, rosewood, and jackfruit trees. This protracted cherry ripening produces low-acidity cup profiles bursting with caramelized sweetness, wild cacao, and toasted hazelnut.”

Why Section-B Roasts Exclusively from the Hills

When we drafted the first recipe cards in notebook Section-B, we refused to settle for mass-market commercial robusta. Every bag of SBC coffee is directly traced to certified high-elevation micro-lots in Chikmagalur (1,200m – 1,450m above sea level). When you sip our 18-hour Biscoff Cold Brew or our single-origin V60, you are tasting an unbroken lineage that started with those seven smuggled seeds in 1670.
