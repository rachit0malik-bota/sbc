CHAPTER 01 · OCTOBER 2021

The Daydream at Desk 14: Escaping the Rote Curriculum

"While the lecture moved through textbook chemistry formulas, we were sketching portafilter pressure hydraulics and sucrose caramelization curves in our notebook margins."

Section-B wasn’t conceived in a venture capitalist boardroom or an upscale design studio. It was born at Desk 14 in Section-B of a Delhi college lecture hall in October 2021.

Our founders, Arpit and his close friends, found themselves caught between two extremes in the Indian coffee landscape: on one side, massive multinational chains serving over-extracted, hyper-sweetened commodity coffee with zero bean traceability; on the other side, third-wave specialty roasteries that felt intimidating, clinical, and pretentious for anyone who didn’t speak fluent barista jargon.

We asked a simple question that became our permanent north star: Why can’t world-class specialty coffee be as welcoming as your high-school classroom common room?

CHAPTER 02 · 1670 HERITAGE & TERROIR

The Seven Smuggled Seeds: Tracing Western Ghats Provenance

"India's coffee history didn't start with corporate imports. It began in 1670 with seven smuggled seeds hidden across a pilgrim's chest into the misty Baba Budan Hills."

To roast extraordinary coffee, we had to go back to where Indian specialty coffee originated. In 1670 AD, the legendary Sufi saint Baba Budan journeyed on a pilgrimage through Yemen. Deeply moved by the aromatic clarity of Coffea Arabica, and knowing the Ottoman Empire strictly forbade exporting raw beans, he wrapped seven viable seeds around his waist.

He planted those seven seeds in the fertile volcanic red soils of the Chandragiri range in Chikmagalur, Karnataka. Today, that mountain range bears his name: the Baba Budan Giri Hills.

Unlike industrial coffee plantations around the globe, Indian specialty Arabica is grown entirely under biodiverse double canopy shade. High-altitude silver oak, wild jackfruit, and black pepper vines filter the tropical sun, allowing coffee cherries to ripen at 1,200 meters elevation across 9 unhurried months. The result is an estate cup with naturally sweet sucrose, low bitterness, and wild chocolate-toffee notes.

CHAPTER 03 · ROAST CHEMISTRY

The 204°C First Crack: Sweetness Over Scorched Ash

"Anyone can roast coffee dark until it tastes like liquid barbecue coal. It takes precision thermochemistry to stop the drum at 204°C and keep the toffee intact."

Commercial coffee roasters often hide low-grade beans by incinerating them into deep Italian or French roasts past 225°C. At that temperature, all natural bean sugars are carbonized into harsh ash and smoky bitterness, forcing consumers to mask the taste with excess sugar and artificial syrups.

At Section-B, our roasting philosophy is strictly centered on sweetness preservation. We roast in small 12kg precision drum batches. As the drum hits 198°C, the moisture inside the cellular matrix rapidly expands until the beans pop like popcorn — the critical event known in specialty roasting as First Crack.

Rather than letting the roast run wild into second crack, our roastmaster arrests the drum at exactly 204°C with immediate ambient air cooling. This freezes the Maillard reactions at peak sucrose caramelization, locking in notes of Belgian speculoos, hazelnut praline, and dark cacao.

CHAPTER 04 · BARISTA HYDRAULICS

9.2-Bar Espresso Hydraulics & The Mascot Meltdown

"A bottomless portafilter never lies. 18 grams in, 36 grams out in 27 seconds of golden tiger-stripe crema."

Behind our slow-bars, extraction is calibrated every morning before the doors open. We dial in temperature-stable dual boilers to 93°C water and 9.2-bar extraction pressure. Using precision 18-gram VST baskets and WDT needle distribution, our baristas eliminate micro-channeling for a syrupy, full-bodied extraction that coats the palate.

It was during an experimental summer slow-bar session in 2022 that our baristas created what would become our signature icon: the Sculpted Teddy Bear Latte. Handcrafting chilled double ristretto espresso into our collegiate mascot bear and placing it inside warm condensed milk and velvety steamed oat milk created an interactive, slowly melting dessert ritual loved across the NCR.
